The Next Decade Will Belong to Intelligent Infrastructure.

Why the companies quietly building the substrate beneath AI will outlast the applications sitting on top of it.

Priya Nandan August 2026 9 min read
On This Page
  • The Shift Underway
  • Why Infrastructure Wins
  • What We're Seeing
  • Where This Leads

Every technology cycle eventually splits into two economies: the layer that captures attention, and the layer that captures value. In artificial intelligence, that split is happening faster than most founders expect.

The applications built on top of today's models are proliferating quickly, and just as quickly, being copied. What's proving far harder to replicate is the infrastructure underneath — the orchestration, retrieval and reliability systems that make intelligence usable at scale.

The Shift Underway

Across our portfolio, we're seeing the same pattern repeat. Companies that started as thin wrappers around a single model are being forced to either go deep on infrastructure or get displaced by whoever does. The moat isn't the model call anymore — it's everything surrounding it.

"The interface layer is a rental. The infrastructure layer is ownership."

Why Infrastructure Wins

Infrastructure businesses compound in ways application businesses rarely do. Switching costs rise with every integration. Reliability becomes a brand. And the teams that build these systems accumulate an operational depth that's difficult for a fast-follower to match, even with more capital.

Infrastructure Share of AI Funding

What We're Seeing

The founders we're most excited about right now aren't chasing the loudest part of the market. They're building the unglamorous, foundational layers — orchestration, evaluation, data pipelines, reliability tooling — that every serious AI company eventually needs.

Where This Leads

We expect the next wave of category-defining companies to look less like consumer AI products and more like the infrastructure firms that powered previous platform shifts. Capital is already moving that direction. Conviction should too.
